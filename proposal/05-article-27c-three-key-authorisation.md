# MEGDA Legislative Redline 05

## Proposed Article 27c
### Three-Key Authorisation for High-Impact AI Deployments

**Status:** Working Draft v0.1  
**Project:** MEGDA — Model for European Governance of Democratic AI  
**Primary legal anchors:** Regulation (EU) 2024/1689, Articles 27, 68, 79, 84, 85 and 86  
**Related MEGDA provisions:** proposed Articles 27a, 27b, 84a and 86a  
**Preferred legislative technique:** insert a new Article 27c after proposed Articles 27a and 27b, with cross-references to Articles 84a and 86a.

---

## 1. Purpose

MEGDA's three-key architecture is designed to prevent a single institution, provider, regulator or technical body from determining alone whether a high-impact AI deployment may proceed.

The three keys answer different questions:

### Democratic Key
**Legitimacy and contestability**

> Is this exercise of algorithmic power proportionate, publicly contestable and legitimate in its social and institutional context?

### Scientific Key
**Evidence**

> Are the material technical claims on which the deployment depends supported by sufficiently independent, representative and reproducible evidence?

### Legal Key
**Responsibility and remedy**

> Is the deployment lawfully attributable to responsible actors and subject to meaningful rights, review and remedy?

The keys are complementary. They are not interchangeable.

# Article 27c
## Three-key authorisation for high-impact AI deployments

### 1. Scope

A deployer shall not commence a deployment classified as having a **high democratic impact** under Article 27a where that deployment falls within a category designated pursuant to paragraph 2, unless the three-key authorisation procedure established by this Article has been completed.

The three-key procedure shall operate in addition to, and shall not replace:

(a) conformity-assessment requirements applicable under this Regulation;

(b) obligations concerning registration, documentation, human oversight, monitoring and incident reporting;

(c) authorisations or procedural requirements arising under other Union or national law.

### 2. Deployments subject to mandatory three-key authorisation

The Commission shall identify categories of high-democratic-impact deployment for which three-key authorisation is mandatory.

The designation shall take account of:

(a) the scale and irreversibility of the effects;

(b) the degree to which the deployment influences access to essential services, benefits, opportunities or rights;

(c) the extent to which the deployment exercises or materially structures public authority;

(d) the vulnerability or dependency of affected persons;

(e) the degree of discretion effectively transferred to or constrained by the AI system;

(f) the availability and practical effectiveness of human review;

(g) the number of persons likely to be affected;

(h) the possibility of cumulative or systemic effects;

(i) the use of AI in democratic, judicial, policing, migration, welfare, healthcare, education, employment or other comparably sensitive institutional contexts.

The designation shall be reviewed periodically and updated where technological or institutional developments materially alter the nature of the risk or power exercised.

### 3. The three required keys

Three-key authorisation shall require three distinct positive findings.

#### (a) Democratic Key

A finding that the requirements of Article 27a have been satisfied, including:

- completion of the democratic impact assessment;
- meaningful public participation;
- reasoned consideration of material concerns;
- assessment of necessity and proportionality;
- identification of accountable institutional responsibility;
- sufficient mechanisms for contestation.

#### (b) Scientific Key

A finding, where Article 84a applies, that independent scientific verification has concluded that the material technical claims necessary for the proposed deployment are supported by evidence that is sufficient or conditionally sufficient within expressly stated limits.

#### (c) Legal Key

A finding that:

- the deployment has an identifiable lawful basis;
- responsibility is attributable to one or more identifiable actors;
- applicable rights to explanation, review, complaint and remedy are operational;
- the requirements of Article 86a are satisfied where applicable;
- no unresolved legal defect prevents deployment.

### 4. Institutional separation

The three keys shall be determined through **functionally independent procedures**.

No single provider, deployer, private contractor or public authority shall be permitted to issue all three keys for the same deployment.

At minimum:

(a) the Scientific Key shall be determined independently from the provider and deployer in accordance with Article 84a;

(b) the Democratic Key shall include participation and reasoned public accountability that cannot be replaced by provider self-assessment;

(c) the Legal Key shall remain subject to competent public authority and judicial review and shall not be determined solely by the provider's or deployer's internal legal assessment.

The Commission shall establish rules preventing organisational arrangements that preserve formal separation while allowing material control of all three determinations by the same actor.

### 5. Coordinating authority

For each deployment subject to this Article, a competent coordinating authority shall be identified.

The coordinating authority shall:

(a) determine whether the three-key procedure applies;

(b) identify the bodies responsible for each required finding;

(c) maintain the procedural timetable;

(d) ensure exchange of information where lawful and necessary;

(e) verify that all three determinations have been completed;

(f) publish the final authorisation status;

(g) ensure that conditions imposed under one key are reflected in the final deployment conditions.

The coordinating authority shall not substitute its own assessment for an independent scientific finding, a required democratic-participation process or the lawful jurisdiction of courts and other competent authorities.

> **Coordination shall not become concentration of substantive power.**

### 6. No deployment with a missing key

A deployment requiring three-key authorisation shall not commence where:

(a) one of the required keys has been refused;

(b) one of the required keys has not been completed;

(c) a material condition attached to a key has not been satisfied;

(d) the information on which a key was based has become materially obsolete before deployment.

Silence, expiry of a procedural deadline or administrative inaction shall not be treated as implicit approval.

### 7. Conditional authorisation

A key may be issued subject to defined conditions where the responsible body concludes that deployment can proceed only within specified limits.

Conditions may concern:

- scale;
- duration;
- affected population;
- human-review requirements;
- technical performance thresholds;
- monitoring and reporting;
- prohibited uses;
- data or model updates;
- additional consultation;
- re-verification.

A conditional key shall identify the condition, the reason, how compliance will be verified, the consequence of non-compliance and the event triggering review.

### 8. Limited pilot authorisation

A limited pilot may be authorised where:

(a) the deployment is strictly bounded in duration, population and purpose;

(b) all three key procedures have been initiated;

(c) no key has identified a defect that would make even limited testing unlawful or unacceptably harmful;

(d) the Scientific Key identifies the pilot as necessary to obtain evidence that cannot reasonably be obtained otherwise;

(e) affected persons receive appropriate notice and safeguards;

(f) meaningful human review remains available;

(g) the competent authority can terminate the pilot immediately.

A pilot shall not be used to create irreversible institutional dependence or to circumvent full three-key authorisation.

Completion of a pilot shall not create a presumption in favour of full deployment.

### 9. Public authorisation record

The final three-key status shall be recorded in a public Union register linked, where applicable, to the database referred to in Article 71.

The public record shall identify:

(a) the deployment;

(b) the deployer;

(c) the bodies responsible for each key;

(d) the status of each key;

(e) material conditions attached to any key;

(f) the date of authorisation;

(g) the date or event requiring reassessment;

(h) the procedure for lodging a complaint or requesting review;

(i) links to the public summaries required under Articles 27a, 84a and 86a.

The register shall distinguish clearly between:

- pending;
- conditionally authorised;
- authorised;
- suspended;
- withdrawn;
- expired.

### 10. Material change and re-authorisation

Three-key authorisation shall be reconsidered where a material change may affect a finding on which one or more keys relied.

Material changes may include:

(a) a substantial change in intended purpose;

(b) deployment to a materially different population;

(c) significant model or system modification;

(d) change in scale;

(e) significant deterioration of performance;

(f) serious incident or newly identified failure mode;

(g) material change in legal basis;

(h) material change in available human review;

(i) evidence that the democratic impact materially differs from the original assessment.

The coordinating authority shall determine which keys require reassessment.

Minor maintenance or technical changes shall not trigger full re-authorisation where they do not materially affect the relevant findings.

### 11. Loss or suspension of a key

Where, after deployment, the conditions supporting a key cease to be satisfied, the body responsible for that key shall inform the coordinating authority without undue delay.

The coordinating authority shall determine whether:

(a) deployment may continue subject to corrective conditions;

(b) the affected function must be limited;

(c) supplementary verification, consultation or legal review is required;

(d) temporary suspension is necessary;

(e) the authorisation must be withdrawn.

Where the deficiency creates a serious and immediate risk, proportionate interim measures may be adopted without awaiting completion of the full reassessment.

### 12. Cross-key escalation

A finding arising under one key may trigger reassessment under another key where materially relevant.

Examples include:

(a) a Scientific Key finding showing substantially different error rates for a vulnerable group may require reconsideration of the Democratic Key;

(b) repeated successful human-review challenges under Article 86a may require supplementary scientific verification under Article 84a;

(c) a material legal change may require reconsideration of the necessity or proportionality analysis under Article 27a;

(d) public consultation may identify a plausible technical claim requiring independent scientific examination.

The purpose of cross-key escalation is to permit information to travel between oversight functions without collapsing those functions into one authority.

### 13. Disagreement between key bodies

Where bodies responsible for different keys reach materially conflicting conclusions, the coordinating authority shall not resolve the conflict merely by selecting one conclusion.

Instead, it shall:

(a) identify the precise point of conflict;

(b) require each body to state the scope and basis of its finding;

(c) determine whether supplementary evidence or procedure can resolve the conflict;

(d) publish a non-confidential summary of the unresolved issue;

(e) prevent full deployment while a required key remains unsatisfied.

Where the conflict concerns a question of Union law, the competent legal or judicial procedures shall prevail.

Where it concerns scientific evidence, the independent scientific process shall remain responsible for the evidentiary finding.

Where it concerns democratic legitimacy, the procedural requirements of Article 27a shall not be substituted by technical or legal opinion alone.

### 14. Review and appeal

A deployer shall have access to an effective procedure to challenge a refusal, condition, suspension or withdrawal of authorisation.

Affected persons and qualified organisations shall have access to applicable complaint and review procedures where they allege that:

(a) a required key was unlawfully omitted;

(b) the three-key process was materially defective;

(c) a deployment materially exceeded the conditions under which it was authorised;

(d) an authority failed to respond to a qualified challenge.

Nothing in this Article shall limit judicial review under Union or national law.

### 15. Anti-capture safeguards

The Commission and Member States shall ensure that the three-key system is designed to resist regulatory, commercial and institutional capture.

Safeguards shall address at least:

(a) conflicts of interest;

(b) revolving-door risks;

(c) concentration of procurement relationships;

(d) repeated dependence on the same private contractors;

(e) provider control of access to evidence;

(f) political interference with scientific findings;

(g) technocratic substitution for democratic participation;

(h) strategic use of consultation to delay or obstruct without substantive engagement;

(i) under-resourcing of independent supervisory bodies;

(j) opacity concerning who made each substantive determination.

No body shall be treated as independent solely because it has a separate legal personality.

Independence shall be assessed in functional and material terms.

### 16. Timeliness

The three-key procedure shall be organised so that meaningful scrutiny does not create avoidable administrative paralysis.

The Commission shall establish procedural benchmarks for coordination between the three keys.

Those benchmarks shall:

(a) permit procedures to run in parallel where this does not compromise independence;

(b) encourage reuse of lawfully shareable evidence;

(c) avoid repeated requests for substantially identical information;

(d) preserve minimum consultation and verification periods necessary for meaningful scrutiny;

(e) require public justification for material procedural delay.

The objective shall be:

> **parallel scrutiny, not sequential bureaucracy.**

### 17. Digital and AI-assisted coordination

AI-assisted tools may be used to support scheduling, document comparison, evidence retrieval, multilingual processing and synthesis across the three-key procedure.

Such tools shall not:

(a) determine whether a key is granted;

(b) rank the substantive importance of democratic objections without human validation;

(c) replace independent scientific judgment;

(d) replace legal responsibility;

(e) conceal disagreement between key bodies.

The use of AI in coordination shall remain auditable and consistent with Article 27b where public deliberation is involved.

### 18. Common Union framework

The Commission, with the support of the AI Office, European Artificial Intelligence Board, Advisory Forum, Scientific Panel and relevant Union fundamental-rights bodies, shall establish a common implementation framework for this Article.

The framework shall include:

- templates for key determinations;
- rules on institutional separation;
- conflict-of-interest safeguards;
- coordination timelines;
- public-register standards;
- material-change thresholds;
- pilot conditions;
- cross-key escalation;
- suspension and withdrawal procedures;
- procedural interoperability with sector-specific law.

The framework shall be developed through an open process involving competent authorities, scientific experts, civil society, workers' representatives, affected communities and industry.

## 3. Constitutional function of Article 27c

Articles 27a, 84a and 86a each create a distinct safeguard.

Article 27c converts them into a **governance architecture**.

Without Article 27c, the keys could remain parallel obligations.

With Article 27c, they become a rule of institutional dependency:

> **No high-impact deployment receives full authorisation unless every required dimension of power has been independently examined.**

---

## 4. Why the keys must not merge

MEGDA rejects the creation of a single "super-authority" that evaluates everything.

The three-key rule deliberately creates friction, but seeks **productive friction**, not bureaucratic duplication.

The system therefore combines:

> **substantive separation + procedural coordination**

rather than:

> **institutional concentration + formal consultation**

---

## 5. What counts as success?

The three-key rule succeeds when:

- evidence can contradict politics;
- law can constrain technical enthusiasm;
- citizens can challenge institutional convenience;
- no actor can silence the others;
- disagreements remain visible;
- the final decision remains attributable to identifiable institutions.

The architecture is not designed to guarantee consensus.

It is designed to make the exercise of technological power **contestable, legible and reversible**.

---

## 6. Relationship to the MEGDA architecture

### Article 27a
**Democratic Impact Assessment**  
Creates the Democratic Key.

### Article 27b
**AI-Assisted Democratic Consultation**  
Makes large-scale participation faster and more intelligible without suppressing plurality.

### Article 84a
**Independent Scientific Verification**  
Creates the Scientific Key.

### Article 86a
**Effective Human Review**  
Creates the core remedial component of the Legal Key.

### Article 27c
**Three-Key Authorisation**  
Connects the keys and prevents deployment when a required key is absent.

## 7. Legal and institutional route

### Preferred legal vehicle

Amend Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 by inserting Article 27c as a new cross-cutting high-impact deployment mechanism.

Because this proposal creates a substantive prior-authorisation layer not generally present in the existing AI Act, it represents a **significant extension** of the current regulatory architecture.

### Primary institutional targets

1. European Commission
2. European Parliament — IMCO and LIBE
3. Council of the European Union / Member States
4. European AI Board
5. AI Office
6. Scientific Panel
7. market-surveillance authorities
8. fundamental-rights and sectoral authorities
