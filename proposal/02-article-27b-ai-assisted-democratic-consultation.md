# Article 27b
## AI-assisted democratic consultation and deliberative transparency

### 1. Principle

Where artificial intelligence is used to assist a consultation conducted pursuant to Article 27a, such use shall serve to **reduce informational complexity without reducing political plurality**.

AI-assisted processing shall not replace the responsibility of natural persons or competent public authorities for the interpretation of submissions, the identification of material concerns or the final decision.

---

### 2. Permitted functions

AI systems may be used to assist with:

(a) semantic clustering of submissions;

(b) identification of recurrent arguments and themes;

(c) comparison of competing proposals;

(d) detection of substantially duplicated submissions;

(e) multilingual translation and linguistic normalisation;

(f) preparation of provisional summaries;

(g) identification of questions requiring further evidence;

(h) construction of publicly accessible maps of areas of agreement, disagreement and uncertainty;

(i) retrieval of submissions supporting a proposition contained in an AI-generated synthesis.

Use of AI for those purposes shall remain subject to human validation and the safeguards in this Article.

---

### 3. Prohibited decisional functions

An AI system used in the consultation process shall not, without meaningful human review:

(a) determine that a submission is politically irrelevant;

(b) exclude a submission solely because its position is uncommon, atypical or supported by a small number of participants;

(c) assign normative weight to an argument solely by reference to its frequency;

(d) determine the admissibility of a public challenge under Article 27a;

(e) determine the outcome of the consultation;

(f) determine whether the proposed deployment is legitimate, necessary or proportionate;

(g) make the final decision to authorise, condition, suspend or reject deployment.

---

### 4. Minority-view safeguard

Consultation synthesis shall distinguish between:

(a) frequency of an argument;

(b) evidentiary strength;

(c) severity of the harm alleged;

(d) the vulnerability or specific exposure of the persons affected;

(e) novelty or uniqueness of the issue raised.

A position shall not be omitted merely because it is numerically minor.

Where an argument is materially relevant to a group likely to experience a disproportionate impact, it shall be separately identified even where few submissions raise it.

---

### 5. Traceability

Every material proposition contained in an AI-assisted public synthesis shall, to the extent compatible with personal-data protection and other applicable confidentiality requirements, be traceable to the underlying submissions or group of submissions from which it was derived.

The consultation interface shall allow a user to distinguish between:

- original submissions;
- machine-generated clustering or labels;
- machine-generated summaries;
- human-validated conclusions;
- the final institutional response.

AI-generated synthesis shall never replace or erase the underlying record.

---

### 6. Preservation of the deliberative record

Original submissions shall be preserved for a period sufficient to permit:

(a) validation of the synthesis;

(b) supervisory review;

(c) audit of the AI-assisted consultation process;

(d) judicial or administrative review where applicable.

Where original submissions cannot lawfully be made public, appropriate protected access mechanisms shall be available to competent authorities and authorised auditors.

---

### 7. Transparency of the consultation AI

The body responsible for the consultation shall publish a non-confidential description of the AI-assisted processing used, including:

(a) the identity of the provider and system or model used, where known and relevant;

(b) the version or materially relevant configuration used;

(c) the functions for which AI was used;

(d) the criteria or instructions materially affecting clustering, summarisation or ranking;

(e) known material limitations;

(f) the human-validation process;

(g) material changes to the system or methodology during the consultation period.

Nothing in this paragraph shall require publication of model weights, source code, protected security information or legitimate trade secrets where disclosure is not necessary to achieve meaningful accountability.

---

### 8. Human validation

AI-generated clustering and synthesis shall be reviewed by one or more natural persons with appropriate competence and independence from the provider of the AI system whose deployment is under consultation.

The validating persons shall specifically examine:

(a) whether minority views have been suppressed or distorted;

(b) whether substantively distinct arguments have been improperly merged;

(c) whether high-frequency coordinated submissions have obscured other material concerns;

(d) whether translation or summarisation has altered the substantive meaning of submissions;

(e) whether relevant counterarguments and uncertainties are adequately represented.

The identity or institutional function of the validating body shall be publicly disclosed.

---

### 9. Public correction mechanism

During the consultation period, participants shall be able to flag a provisional AI-generated summary or classification that they reasonably consider to materially misrepresent a submission or category of submissions.

Material correction requests and their disposition shall be recorded.

A correction request shall not automatically alter the synthesis, but shall receive meaningful human review.

---

### 10. Continuous synthesis

For consultations exceeding 15 days, the responsible body should, where technically feasible, publish periodically updated provisional syntheses during the consultation period.

Those syntheses shall clearly state that they are provisional and shall not be treated as an indication of the eventual decision.

The purpose of continuous synthesis is to allow participants to:

(a) understand the emerging debate;

(b) identify arguments that may have been overlooked;

(c) respond to competing positions;

(d) contribute evidence to unresolved questions before the consultation closes.

---

### 11. Final synthesis timetable

The final AI-assisted synthesis should normally be available at or shortly after the close of consultation.

Human validation and publication of the validated synthesis should normally be completed within **10 days** after closure.

Where the complexity or volume of submissions makes compliance impossible, the responsible body shall publish the reason for delay and a revised timetable.

The use of AI-assisted synthesis shall be organised with the objective of preventing avoidable administrative delay.

---

### 12. Participation shall not become a popularity contest

The outcome of a democratic consultation shall not be determined by a simple numerical aggregation of submissions unless Union or national law expressly provides for a vote.

The purpose of consultation under Article 27a is deliberative and evidentiary.

The responsible authority shall consider the substance, distributional consequences and rights implications of the arguments received, not merely their number.

---

### 13. Coordinated and automated submissions

The consultation body may use proportionate technical measures to identify substantially duplicated, automated or coordinated submissions.

Such identification shall be used to improve transparency and analysis and shall not, by itself, justify disregarding the substance of the submissions.

Legitimate collective mobilisation, campaigning or the use of common templates shall not be treated as evidence that the underlying position lacks democratic relevance.

---

### 14. Auditability

The AI-assisted consultation process shall generate an audit record sufficient to reconstruct:

(a) what data were processed;

(b) what AI system and configuration were used;

(c) what material processing steps were performed;

(d) what human interventions altered or validated the output;

(e) what corrections were made;

(f) how the final public synthesis was produced.

Competent authorities shall have access to that record where necessary for supervision or review.

---

### 15. Common technical and procedural standards

The AI Office, in cooperation with the European Artificial Intelligence Board, the Advisory Forum, the Scientific Panel, relevant fundamental-rights bodies and representatives of civil society, shall develop common standards for AI-assisted democratic consultation.

Those standards shall address at least:

- traceability;
- minority-view preservation;
- multilingual consultation;
- accessibility;
- human validation;
- documentation;
- auditability;
- coordinated submissions;
- bias and representational distortion;
- correction mechanisms;
- cybersecurity;
- protection of personal data;
- publication of provisional and final syntheses.

---

## MEGDA constitutional principle

> **AI-assisted deliberation shall reduce informational complexity, not political plurality.**

The technology may accelerate reading, comparison, translation and synthesis.

It may not decide which citizens matter.

---

## Relationship to Article 27a

Article 27a creates a right to meaningful participation for specified high-democratic-impact deployments.

Article 27b makes that participation scalable.

The intended cycle is:

**raw submissions**  
→ **AI-assisted clustering and synthesis**  
→ **public traceability and correction**  
→ **human validation**  
→ **reasoned institutional response**  
→ **reviewable decision**

The speed benefit comes from using AI to compress administrative processing time, not from shortening the public's opportunity to participate without justification.

---

## Why this matters

Without computational assistance, large consultations can produce a paradox:

- more participation generates more information;
- more information creates administrative delay;
- delay makes participation less consequential.

MEGDA uses AI to break that cycle while imposing safeguards against a different risk: allowing the synthesis tool itself to become a hidden political gatekeeper.
