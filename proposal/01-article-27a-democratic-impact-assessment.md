# MEGDA Legislative Redline 01

## Article 27 → Proposed Article 27a
### Democratic Impact Assessment for High-Impact AI Deployments

**Status:** Working Draft v0.3  
**Project:** MEGDA — Model for European Governance of Democratic AI  
**Primary legal anchor:** Regulation (EU) 2024/1689, Article 27  
**Preferred legislative technique:** retain Article 27 and insert a new Article 27a immediately after it.

---

## 1. Current legal anchor

Article 27 of the AI Act requires specified deployers of certain high-risk AI systems to carry out a fundamental rights impact assessment before deployment. The assessment covers, among other matters, the context and frequency of use, the categories of persons likely to be affected, specific risks of harm, human oversight and measures to be taken if risks materialise.

Article 27 also allows reliance on previous assessments in similar cases, requires updating when relevant elements change, and requires notification of the results to the market-surveillance authority.

### MEGDA reading

Article 27 already recognises that deployment of certain AI systems is not merely a technical act. It may materially affect the practical exercise of fundamental rights.

MEGDA adds a second question:

> **How much institutional or social power does this deployment exercise over people, and how can the people affected meaningfully contest its introduction?**

# Article 27a
## Democratic impact assessment for high-impact AI deployments

### 1. General obligation

Where a deployer referred to in Article 27(1) intends to deploy a high-risk AI system whose use is reasonably likely to have a **high democratic impact**, the deployer shall, prior to deployment, carry out a democratic impact assessment as an integral and distinguishable part of the fundamental rights impact assessment referred to in Article 27.

The obligations under this Article shall be without prejudice to any more protective obligations arising from Union or national law.

---

### 2. High democratic impact

A deployment shall be considered to have a high democratic impact where, having regard to its purpose, scale, context, reversibility and reasonably foreseeable effects, it is capable of materially affecting one or more of the following:

(a) access to an essential public service, social benefit, educational opportunity, employment opportunity, healthcare service or other service of comparable importance;

(b) the exercise of a fundamental right or the practical ability of natural persons to exercise that right;

(c) the exercise of public authority, including prioritisation, classification, ranking, assessment or differential treatment of natural persons;

(d) a substantial category of natural persons or a group in a position of vulnerability;

(e) the effective ability of natural persons to obtain meaningful human review, contest a decision or use a non-automated procedure where such a procedure is required by Union or national law;

(f) democratic participation, civic pluralism, access to public information or the conditions under which persons participate in public life;

(g) the distribution of institutional decision-making power in a manner that significantly reduces effective human discretion or concentrates material decision-making influence in an AI-mediated process.

---

### 3. Combined determination of high democratic impact

The determination referred to in paragraph 2 shall operate through a **combined assessment mechanism** consisting of:

#### (a) Statutory triggers

The Commission shall identify, by delegated act adopted in accordance with Article 97, categories of deployment that shall be presumed to have a high democratic impact.

Such categories shall include, where appropriate, deployments concerning essential public services, social protection, healthcare, education, employment, justice, democratic processes and other uses involving comparable exercises of public or quasi-public power.

#### (b) Deployer self-assessment

Before deployment, the deployer shall document whether the proposed use meets, or may reasonably meet, the criteria in paragraph 2.

Where the deployer concludes that the deployment does not have a high democratic impact, that conclusion shall be reasoned, documented and retained for supervisory review.

#### (c) Independent authority determination

The competent market-surveillance authority may confirm, revise or require reconsideration of the deployer's classification.

Where there is reasonable doubt, the authority shall interpret the classification criteria in a manner that preserves effective scrutiny of deployments capable of materially affecting fundamental rights or democratic participation.

#### (d) Public challenge

Affected persons, representative organisations, workers' representatives, civil-society organisations and other qualified entities shall be entitled to submit a reasoned challenge to the classification.

A challenge shall not itself determine the classification, but shall require the competent authority to assess its admissibility and, where the challenge raises a plausible issue of high democratic impact, to issue a reasoned determination.

The Commission shall establish common procedural criteria preventing both abusive mass-triggering and unjustified institutional refusal to examine qualified challenges.

---

### 4. Contents of the democratic impact assessment

In addition to the elements required under Article 27, the democratic impact assessment shall include at least:

(a) a clear description of the objective pursued by the deployment;

(b) an assessment of the **necessity and proportionality** of using an AI system for that objective;

(c) a description of reasonably available non-AI or less intrusive alternatives and the reasons for rejecting them;

(d) an assessment of how benefits, burdens, errors and risks are expected to be distributed among affected persons and groups;

(e) the degree of discretion or decision-making authority effectively transferred to, or materially influenced by, the AI system;

(f) the identity, function and authority of the natural person or public body that remains accountable for decisions materially influenced by the AI system;

(g) the mechanisms available to affected persons to request information, obtain review, lodge a complaint and challenge the use or output of the system;

(h) the conditions under which use of the system shall be reduced, suspended or terminated;

(i) the measures ensuring continuity of the relevant service where the AI system is unavailable, suspended or withdrawn.

---

### 5. Public participation

Before deployment, the deployer shall publish, in an accessible form, a non-confidential summary of the proposed deployment and of the preliminary democratic impact assessment.

Affected persons and groups, their representative organisations, workers' representatives where relevant, civil-society organisations and independent experts shall be given a **meaningful opportunity to submit observations**.

The consultation shall follow one of the following procedures:

(a) **accelerated procedure — 15 days**, for a deployment of limited scale or for a material modification of a deployment already subject to a democratic impact assessment, where the competent authority has approved the accelerated procedure;

(b) **standard procedure — 30 days**, for ordinary high-democratic-impact deployments;

(c) **extended procedure — between 45 and 60 days**, where the scale, cross-border character, irreversibility, novelty or sensitivity of the deployment requires a longer deliberative period.

Any decision to apply an accelerated or extended procedure shall be reasoned and made publicly available.

An extension beyond 60 days shall be exceptional and shall require a specific public justification.

---

### 6. Timeliness as a democratic safeguard

Procedural delay shall not be used to frustrate effective participation or to render consultation practically irrelevant.

The deployer and the competent authority shall organise the assessment so that democratic scrutiny is proportionate both in **depth and in time**.

Digital and AI-assisted tools may be used to reduce informational and administrative complexity in accordance with Article 27b.

---

### 7. Reasoned response

The deployer shall assess the observations received and publish a reasoned response explaining:

(a) which material concerns were accepted;

(b) which material concerns were rejected and why;

(c) which modifications, safeguards or conditions were introduced as a consequence of consultation;

(d) whether, following consultation, the deployer continues to consider the deployment necessary and proportionate.

The response shall form part of the democratic impact assessment.

---

### 8. Prior notification and review

The completed democratic impact assessment and the reasoned response shall be submitted to the competent market-surveillance authority before deployment.

Where the assessment is manifestly incomplete, the participation procedure materially defective, or the proposed deployment raises a serious risk to fundamental rights or the effective exercise of democratic rights, the authority may:

(a) require supplementary information or additional safeguards;

(b) require the deployer to repeat or extend participation;

(c) require modifications to the conditions of deployment;

(d) require that deployment not commence until identified deficiencies have been remedied, in accordance with applicable Union law and the authority's lawful powers.

Any such measure shall be reasoned, proportionate, time-limited where appropriate and subject to judicial review.

---

### 9. Publication and Union database

Where the relevant system or deployment is subject to registration under this Regulation, a non-confidential summary of the following shall be made available through the EU database referred to in Article 71:

(a) the democratic impact assessment;

(b) the principal observations received;

(c) the deployer's reasoned response;

(d) any material conditions or corrective measures imposed by the competent authority.

Protected personal data, legitimate trade secrets, confidential supervisory information and information whose disclosure would create a demonstrable risk to public security shall not be published.

Restrictions on publication shall be interpreted narrowly and shall not prevent disclosure of information necessary to understand the purpose, institutional responsibility, principal risks and means of contestation associated with the deployment.

---

### 10. Reliance on previous assessments

A deployer relying on a previous assessment shall document why the previous and proposed deployments are **materially equivalent** with regard to:

(a) purpose;

(b) affected population;

(c) institutional context;

(d) scale and frequency of use;

(e) degree of human discretion;

(f) reasonably foreseeable risks and distributional effects.

Where material equivalence cannot be demonstrated, a new democratic impact assessment shall be carried out.

---

### 11. Urgent deployments

Where an overriding and duly justified public interest makes completion of the ordinary participation procedure before deployment impossible, Union or national law may provide for a strictly limited urgent procedure.

Any derogation shall:

(a) be interpreted restrictively;

(b) state the reasons for urgency;

(c) be limited in duration and scope;

(d) preserve safeguards necessary to prevent serious or irreversible harm;

(e) require the participatory procedure and full assessment to be completed without undue delay.

Urgency resulting primarily from administrative delay, procurement planning or foreseeable organisational constraints shall not, by itself, justify this procedure.

---

### 12. Common methodology

The AI Office, in cooperation with the European Artificial Intelligence Board, the Advisory Forum, the Scientific Panel and relevant Union bodies competent in fundamental rights, shall develop a common methodology and template.

The methodology shall address:

- scale;
- reversibility;
- vulnerability;
- degree of automated influence;
- availability of meaningful human review;
- concentration of institutional decision-making power;
- cumulative effects;
- equality;
- pluralism;
- access to essential services;
- criteria for accelerated, standard and extended consultation;
- criteria for qualified public challenges.

The methodology shall be developed through an open and participatory process.

---

## Why a new Article 27a?

Article 27 already has a clear legal function: a fundamental-rights impact assessment.

MEGDA would preserve that structure and add a separate democratic layer.

> **Article 27:** What can this deployment do to fundamental rights?  
> **Article 27a:** What power does this deployment exercise over people, and how can affected people contest its introduction?

---

## Legislative route

**Preferred legal vehicle:** amendment to Regulation (EU) 2024/1689.

Article 112 requires the Commission to evaluate, by 2 August 2028 and every four years thereafter, possible amendments enhancing the effectiveness of the supervision and governance system. That review process is a natural institutional entry point for MEGDA.

**Primary targets:** European Parliament (IMCO/LIBE), European Commission, Council / Member States, European AI Board, Advisory Forum and relevant fundamental-rights bodies.
