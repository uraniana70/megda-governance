# MEGDA Legislative Redline 06

## Article 79 → Proposed Article 79a
### Distributed Emergency Review and Temporary Suspension

**Status:** Working Draft v0.1  
**Project:** MEGDA — Model for European Governance of Democratic AI  
**Primary legal anchors:** Regulation (EU) 2024/1689, Articles 26, 73 and 79  
**Related MEGDA provisions:** proposed Articles 27a, 27b, 27c, 84a and 86a  
**Preferred legislative technique:** retain Article 79 and insert a new Article 79a immediately after it.

---

## 1. Current legal anchors

### Article 26 — deployer suspension duty

Where deployers of high-risk AI systems have reason to consider that use of the system may result in a risk within the meaning of Article 79(1), they must inform the provider or distributor and the relevant market-surveillance authority and suspend use.

### Article 73 — serious-incident reporting

Providers of high-risk AI systems must report serious incidents to the competent market-surveillance authorities.

### Article 79 — national procedure for AI systems presenting a risk

Where a market-surveillance authority has sufficient reason to consider that an AI system presents a risk, it evaluates the system and its compliance and may require corrective action.

### MEGDA reading

The AI Act already contains the basic logic:

> **signal → investigation → corrective action → restriction**

MEGDA adds a distributed trigger architecture so that emergency review does not depend on a single institutional gatekeeper.

# Article 79a
## Distributed emergency review and temporary suspension

### 1. Purpose

This Article establishes a rapid, distributed procedure for the review of a high-impact AI deployment where credible information indicates a serious and potentially urgent risk.

The procedure shall be designed to:

(a) permit several independent actors to trigger scrutiny;

(b) prevent a provider, deployer or single authority from suppressing a credible warning;

(c) preserve the lawful decision-making powers of competent public authorities;

(d) allow proportionate temporary measures before irreversible harm occurs;

(e) ensure rapid judicial and administrative review.

### 2. Emergency-review signals

An emergency-review signal may originate from:

(a) the competent market-surveillance authority;

(b) the AI Office, within its lawful competence;

(c) the Scientific Panel or an appropriate Union AI testing support structure;

(d) a serious-incident report under Article 73;

(e) a deployer notification or suspension under Article 26;

(f) a qualified scientific challenge under Article 84a;

(g) a qualified collective or civic challenge relating to a deployment subject to Article 27a or 27c;

(h) another competent public authority responsible for fundamental rights, data protection, equality, labour, consumer protection, public safety or sector-specific supervision.

A signal shall identify, to the extent reasonably possible, the deployment concerned, the nature of the suspected risk and the evidence or circumstances supporting the concern.

### 3. Threshold for emergency review

The competent market-surveillance authority shall open an emergency review without undue delay where:

(a) one signal contains sufficiently credible evidence of a serious and immediate risk; or

(b) two or more **functionally independent signal sources** identify materially related concerns concerning the same deployment.

For the purposes of point (b), two signals shall not be treated as independent where they are materially controlled by the same provider, deployer, contractor or authority.

### 4. Distributed trigger — no automatic civic veto

A qualified civic, scientific or institutional signal may trigger review but shall not, by itself, impose a coercive suspension.

Where two independent signal sources converge on a plausible serious risk, the competent authority shall:

(a) open emergency review;

(b) determine without undue delay whether interim measures are required;

(c) publish a non-confidential notice that the review has been opened, unless publication would itself create a material risk to security, an investigation or protected rights.

Where the authority decides not to impose an interim measure despite two independent convergent signals, it shall issue a reasoned decision.

### 5. Temporary precautionary suspension

The competent authority may order temporary suspension, limitation or modification of the deployment where available information provides reasonable grounds to consider that continued operation during review may create:

(a) serious or irreversible harm to health, safety or fundamental rights;

(b) material and widespread denial of access to an essential service, benefit or opportunity;

(c) serious discriminatory or systematically unequal effects;

(d) loss of meaningful human control or review;

(e) serious interference with democratic participation or public institutional integrity;

(f) a serious technical failure or material performance degradation that cannot immediately be contained;

(g) another risk of comparable gravity.

The measure shall be necessary, proportionate and no broader than required to address the suspected risk.

### 6. Two-source presumption for interim action

Where at least two functionally independent sources among the following concur that continued operation creates a serious and immediate risk:

1. competent market-surveillance authority;
2. AI Office, where competent;
3. Scientific Panel or Union AI testing support structure;
4. another legally competent independent public authority;

there shall be a rebuttable presumption in favour of an interim restrictive measure.

The competent authority may decline to impose such a measure only by a reasoned decision explaining why immediate restriction would be unnecessary or disproportionate.

This paragraph shall not transfer coercive powers to advisory or scientific bodies.

### 7. Civic escalation

Where a qualified civic challenge is supported by evidence of serious harm or by a defined threshold of affected persons or representative organisations, the competent authority shall assess it under an accelerated admissibility procedure.

Where the challenge is found admissible and is independently corroborated by:

(a) a competent public authority;

(b) a Scientific Panel or testing-structure finding;

(c) a serious-incident report;

(d) documented repeated successful human-review challenges under Article 86a;

the competent authority shall open emergency review.

The Commission shall establish common criteria for qualified civic challenges so as to preserve accessibility while limiting manifestly abusive or fraudulent triggering.

### 8. Immediate evidence preservation

Upon opening emergency review, the competent authority may require preservation of information necessary to reconstruct the operation of the system.

Such information may include, where relevant:

(a) logs;

(b) system-version information;

(c) relevant prompts, inputs or output records where lawfully retainable;

(d) monitoring records;

(e) incident reports;

(f) human-review records;

(g) relevant technical documentation;

(h) records of material configuration changes.

Evidence-preservation requirements shall comply with applicable data-protection, confidentiality and security law.

### 9. Rapid scientific assessment

Where the suspected risk raises a material technical question, the competent authority may request accelerated scientific assessment under Article 84a.

The assessment shall distinguish between:

(a) established findings;

(b) plausible but unresolved risks;

(c) unsupported allegations;

(d) limitations caused by insufficient access or evidence.

Scientific uncertainty shall not automatically prevent a proportionate interim measure where the reasonably foreseeable harm is severe or irreversible.

### 10. Emergency democratic review

Where the suspected risk materially changes the social effects, affected population, purpose or distributional consequences considered under Article 27a, the Democratic Key shall be reopened to the extent necessary.

Emergency democratic review shall be proportionate to urgency.

It may use accelerated consultation, representative hearings or other methods that preserve meaningful participation without requiring repetition of the entire original procedure.

### 11. Emergency legal review

Where the suspected risk concerns lawful authority, fundamental rights, responsibility, reviewability or the practical effectiveness of remedies, the competent legal authority shall examine whether the Legal Key remains satisfied.

Where a defect can be remedied immediately through a narrower lawful measure, the least restrictive effective remedy should be preferred to complete suspension.

### 12. Time limits for interim measures

A temporary suspension under this Article shall be granted for a defined period.

An initial emergency measure should not normally exceed **30 days** without a reasoned renewal.

Renewal shall require:

(a) evidence that the risk remains unresolved;

(b) an explanation of investigative progress;

(c) review of whether a narrower measure is now sufficient;

(d) a revised timetable for final determination.

Urgent protective measures may take effect immediately where delay would materially increase the risk of serious harm.

### 13. Final emergency-review decision

At the conclusion of emergency review, the competent authority shall issue a reasoned decision that may:

(a) lift the interim measure;

(b) permit continued deployment subject to corrective conditions;

(c) require supplementary scientific verification;

(d) require renewed democratic assessment or consultation;

(e) require changes to human-review or remedy procedures;

(f) restrict specified functions or uses;

(g) suspend the deployment for a defined period;

(h) withdraw or terminate authorisation where legally permitted.

The decision shall identify the evidence relied upon and the status of each affected MEGDA key.

### 14. Public emergency record

A non-confidential public record shall identify:

(a) that emergency review was opened;

(b) the category of trigger or triggers;

(c) interim measures adopted;

(d) principal reasons, to the extent publication is lawful;

(e) the final disposition;

(f) any conditions for resumption;

(g) any requirement for re-authorisation under Article 27c.

Protected personal data, trade secrets, confidential supervisory information and security-sensitive information shall not be disclosed.

Restrictions shall not prevent publication of the basic fact that a deployment has been suspended, restricted or placed under emergency review where such disclosure is necessary for affected persons to understand their rights.

### 15. Protection of whistleblowers and good-faith reporters

No person shall suffer retaliation solely for making, in good faith, a report or challenge concerning a plausible serious AI-related risk.

This Article shall be without prejudice to applicable Union and national whistleblower-protection law.

Knowingly fabricated evidence or manifestly abusive use of the emergency procedure may be addressed under proportionate applicable law.

### 16. Relationship with Article 27c

For deployments subject to three-key authorisation:

(a) temporary suspension under this Article shall place the relevant public authorisation status into **suspended** or **under emergency review** status;

(b) a finding that one key is no longer satisfied shall trigger the procedure in Article 27c(11);

(c) resumption shall require confirmation that all keys materially affected by the incident remain satisfied.

### 17. Judicial and administrative review

Providers and deployers shall have access to effective review of restrictive measures.

Affected persons and qualified organisations shall have access to applicable procedures to challenge:

(a) an unlawful failure to open emergency review;

(b) manifestly inadequate interim protection;

(c) unlawful continuation of a deployment after a required key has ceased to be satisfied.

Nothing in this Article shall limit access to judicial review under Union or national law.

### 18. Anti-capture rule

Emergency review shall not depend exclusively on information supplied by the provider or deployer whose system is under review.

Competent authorities shall be able, where necessary and lawful, to obtain information from:

- independent researchers;
- affected persons;
- workers and their representatives;
- civil-society organisations;
- other regulators;
- independent scientific bodies;
- auditors and testing structures.

No actor shall be able to neutralise an emergency signal solely by controlling access to the evidence necessary to investigate it.

### 19. Common Union procedure

The Commission, with the support of the AI Office, European AI Board, Scientific Panel, relevant fundamental-rights bodies and market-surveillance authorities, shall establish common procedural standards for this Article.

Those standards shall address:

- signal admissibility;
- functional independence of signal sources;
- urgency classification;
- temporary measures;
- evidence preservation;
- cross-border coordination;
- civic escalation;
- scientific uncertainty;
- public notices;
- procedural timelines;
- renewal and termination of interim measures;
- interaction with Article 79 and the Union safeguard procedure.

## 3. Why a new Article 79a?

Article 26 already requires deployers to suspend use when they have reason to consider that a high-risk system may present an Article 79 risk.

Article 73 already creates serious-incident reporting.

Article 79 already provides the competent-authority procedure for systems presenting a risk.

MEGDA adds a missing institutional question:

> **Who can force the system to look when the ordinary gatekeeper does not want to look?**

The answer is not to give coercive power to everyone.

It is to distribute **trigger power** while keeping **restrictive legal power** in accountable public institutions.

---

## 4. The emergency-brake formula

MEGDA distinguishes three levels:

### Signal
Many actors may raise the alarm.

### Mandatory review
One sufficiently serious signal, or two independent convergent signals, can oblige the competent authority to examine the issue.

### Coercive suspension
Only a legally competent authority may impose the binding restrictive measure, subject to reasons and review.

This avoids both extremes:

> **centralised institutional silence**

and

> **unaccountable popular veto**

---

## 5. Relationship to the three-key architecture

A serious incident may reveal failure in any key.

### Scientific failure
The system does not perform as represented.

### Legal failure
The system's use is unlawful or remedies cease to be effective.

### Democratic failure
The deployment produces materially different social consequences from those on which authorisation was based.

Article 79a creates the mechanism that can rapidly reopen whichever key has become unstable.
