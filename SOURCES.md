# Sources and legal anchors

This file records the primary legal anchors used in the interactive prototype.

## EU AI Act

**Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 — consolidated version, 27 July 2026**

Official consolidated text:

https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2024/1689/2026-07-27/eng

Key provisions referenced by the prototype:

- **Article 6** — classification rules for high-risk AI systems.
- **Article 14** — human oversight for high-risk systems.
- **Article 26** — obligations of deployers of high-risk AI systems.
- **Article 27** — fundamental-rights impact assessment for specified high-risk deployments.
- **Articles 49 and 71** — registration duties and the EU database for high-risk AI systems.
- **Articles 64–68** — AI Office, European AI Board, Advisory Forum and Scientific Panel of independent experts.
- **Articles 72–73** — post-market monitoring and serious-incident reporting.
- **Article 79** — procedure for AI systems presenting a risk.
- **Article 84** — Union AI testing support structures.
- **Article 85** — right to lodge a complaint with a market-surveillance authority.
- **Article 86** — right to explanation of individual decision-making in defined circumstances.

## Interpretation rule used in the prototype

The interface distinguishes three categories:

### Build from current Act

The existing AI Act already provides a sufficiently close legal or institutional mechanism that implementation could begin primarily through capacity, procedure, funding or implementing measures.

### AI Act amendment

The MEGDA proposal is conceptually connected to an existing AI Act mechanism, but would require legislative change to create new rights, triggers, duties or institutional powers.

### Parallel / new instrument

The proposal concerns a structural issue that the AI Act was not designed to govern directly — especially compute infrastructure or cross-market concentration of technological power — and is likely to require other EU legal bases or a separate instrument.

## Methodological note

MEGDA is a policy-design project. The mapping from current law to proposed legal vehicle is analytical and should be tested by EU-law specialists before being treated as a legislative drafting position.
