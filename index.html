Where MEGDA comes from: thirteen legal anchors and extensions.

MEGDA does not invent its vocabulary from nothing. Its mechanisms begin with duties already present in the AI Act: human oversight, fundamental-rights assessment, registration, independent expertise, complaints, explanations, post-market monitoring and risk intervention. MEGDA changes their constitutional function: it turns scattered regulatory duties into a proposed separation of technological powers. Open any provision to see the current rule, the gap, the MEGDA extension, the likely legal vehicle and the institution that could carry it.

Adoption logic

Do not send MEGDA to one gatekeeper.

The implementation path mirrors the governance principle itself: distribute initiative across institutions so no single actor can bury, own or neutralise the proposal.

1 — Civic coalitionAcademia, civil society, labour, SMEs, researchers and citizens.
2 — ParliamentIMCO/LIBE working structures, hearings, own-initiative work and legislative pressure.
3 — Member StatesNational governments and competent authorities create Council-side pressure.
4 — Citizen routeEuropean Citizens' Initiative or other participatory channels build bottom-up legitimacy.
5 — CommissionConvergence point for legislative proposal, implementation and AI Office governance.

Two MEGDA components — the Public Compute Commons and Systemic AI Power — have no direct textual anchor in the AI Act. The Commons belongs mainly to EuroHPC and EU industrial/infrastructure policy; Systemic AI Power belongs closer to competition law, the Digital Markets Act and strategic-infrastructure governance.

This is a policy-design and legal-architecture prototype, not legal advice. Article descriptions are deliberately concise and should be read alongside the official consolidated text.
