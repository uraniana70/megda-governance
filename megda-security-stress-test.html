MEGDA · Model for European Governance of Democratic AI
Security review · 10 minutes

Can you break this governance model?

MEGDA proposes that, for high-impact AI, creation, verification, authorisation and contestation should not be controlled by the same actor. This is not a request for a philosophical opinion. It is a request for a security-minded attack on the architecture.

Think of MEGDA as a separation-of-duties model for algorithmic power: independent verification, multi-key authorisation, human review, escalation and emergency suspension.
01 · Single point of failure

Where would you attack first?

Which component could become a single point of failure, trusted bottleneck or hidden dependency? What would you remove, duplicate or isolate?

02 · Separation of duties

Can the three keys be captured?

MEGDA separates Democratic, Scientific and Legal authorisation. How could one organisation, vendor or network influence two or all three in practice?

03 · Escalation

What must force a review?

Which signals should trigger mandatory escalation: anomalous behaviour, repeated complaints, model change, security incident, audit failure, or something else?

04 · Evidence

What must be logged?

After an incident, what evidence is essential to reconstruct who knew what, when, and who authorised what? Which logs must be immutable or independently retained?

05 · Emergency brake

How could suspension fail?

MEGDA allows distributed signals but keeps coercive suspension with accountable public authorities. Where could delay, spoofing, denial-of-service or institutional capture defeat the brake?

The useful answer is not “I like it”. The useful answer is: “This is where it can be bypassed, captured, spoofed or made operationally useless.”
