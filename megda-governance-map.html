This module turns the legislative proposals into a readable institutional flow. It shows what happens before deployment, what must converge for authorisation, and what happens after deployment if something goes wrong. Click any article to inspect its role in the architecture.

27a, 84a and 86a establish the Democratic, Scientific and Legal Keys. 27b makes democratic participation scalable rather than slow and unreadable.
Article 27a Democratic Key

Democratic Impact Assessment

Creates structured public participation, proportional consultation and pre-deployment democratic review.

Who is affected? What are the stakes? Is the deployment proportionate?
Article 27b Democratic support

AI-Assisted Democratic Consultation

Uses AI to accelerate consultation and synthesis while preserving pluralism, traceability and human validation.

Parallel scrutiny, not deliberative automation.
Article 84a Scientific Key

Independent Scientific Verification

Tests material technical claims through independent verification, qualified challenges and public scientific statements.

Can the evidence support the system's claimed performance?
Article 86a Legal Key

Effective Human Review

Guarantees meaningful human reconsideration, accountable reviewers, interim safeguards and reasoned outcomes.

Can people challenge and reverse consequential decisions?

Article 27c Three-Key Rule

Three-Key Authorisation

Connects the Democratic, Scientific and Legal Keys into one prior-authorisation framework for designated high-impact deployments.

Article 79a Emergency Brake

Distributed Emergency Review

Allows multiple independent actors to trigger scrutiny while keeping binding suspension powers in competent public authorities.

Signal → mandatory review → coercive suspension.

Article 27a — Democratic Impact Assessment

    Sequence at a glance
    1. Build the keys 27a creates democratic scrutiny, 84a creates scientific scrutiny, and 86a creates legal contestability. 27b helps scale democratic consultation.
    2. Converge the keys 27c requires the relevant keys to be independently satisfied before designated high-impact AI deployments may proceed.
    3. Reopen the system if needed 79a distributes trigger power for emergency review when serious incidents, new evidence or civic challenges show that a key may have failed.
